# Small Business Cybersecurity Awareness Training

### Based on guidance from the Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC)

Duration: \~20--22 minutes\
Slides: 23 (Core Training + Interactive Quiz)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 1 -- Cybersecurity for Australian Small Business

**Slide Content:** - Based on ACSC guidance\
- Practical. Clear. Actionable.\
- For everyone in the business

**Narrator Transcript:** Welcome.

This training is designed specifically for Australian small businesses.

It is based on guidance from the Australian Cyber Security Centre, or
ACSC.

Today is not about technical jargon.

It is about understanding how businesses like yours get compromised...\
and what every single person can do to reduce that risk.

Because cybersecurity is everyone's responsibility.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 2 -- Why Cybersecurity Matters

**Slide Content:** - Small businesses are heavily targeted\
- Most attacks are opportunistic\
- Financial and reputational impact

**Narrator Transcript:** Many small businesses believe...

"We're too small to be targeted."

In reality, small businesses are targeted more often than large
enterprises.

Attackers look for easy opportunities.

They care about weak passwords...\
unpatched software...\
and untrained staff.

Most incidents are preventable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 3 -- The Most Common Compromise Methods

**Slide Content:** - Phishing\
- Business Email Compromise\
- Ransomware\
- Stolen passwords\
- Unpatched systems

**Narrator Transcript:** According to the ACSC, most business
compromises begin in predictable ways.

Phishing emails.\
Business Email Compromise.\
Ransomware.\
Stolen passwords.\
Unpatched systems.

These attacks rely on behaviour --- not sophistication.

That means they can be prevented.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 4 -- Phishing -- The #1 Entry Point

**Slide Content:** - Fake trusted emails\
- Fake login pages\
- Malicious attachments

**Narrator Transcript:** Phishing is the number one entry point into
Australian businesses.

It looks like a normal email.

A supplier.\
A courier.\
Your bank.\
Or your manager.

The email asks you to click a link or download a file.

The link leads to a fake login page.

You enter your password.

Your account is now compromised.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 5 -- Business Email Compromise

**Slide Content:** - Bank detail change scams\
- Fake urgent payments\
- Account takeover fraud

**Narrator Transcript:** Business Email Compromise is one of the most
financially damaging scams.

An attacker gains access to an email account.

They monitor conversations quietly.

Then at the right moment...

They request a payment or change bank details.

Always verify bank detail changes using a known phone number.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 6 -- Ransomware

**Slide Content:** - Files encrypted\
- Payment demanded\
- Operations halted

**Narrator Transcript:** Ransomware locks your files and demands
payment.

It often starts with phishing or an exposed system.

Even if payment is made, recovery is not guaranteed.

Backups are critical.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 7 -- Stolen Passwords & MFA Fatigue

**Slide Content:** - Password reuse risk\
- Data breach exposure\
- Repeated approval requests

**Narrator Transcript:** If you reuse passwords and one site is
breached...

Attackers try that password everywhere.

Never approve a login you did not initiate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 8 -- Unpatched Software

**Slide Content:** - Security vulnerabilities\
- Known exploits\
- Automated scanning

**Narrator Transcript:** Updates fix security vulnerabilities.

Enable automatic updates wherever possible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 9 -- What is the Essential Eight?

**Slide Content:** - Developed by the ACSC\
- Eight mitigation strategies\
- Designed to reduce compromise

**Narrator Transcript:** The Essential Eight was developed by the ACSC.

It outlines eight key strategies that significantly reduce the risk of
cyber compromise.

It is based on real-world Australian attack patterns.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 10 -- Keep Systems Updated

**Slide Content:** - Enable automatic updates\
- Patch operating systems\
- Patch applications

**Narrator Transcript:** Updates close security gaps.

Delaying updates increases risk.

Enable automatic updates wherever possible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 11 -- Only Use Approved Software

**Slide Content:** - Avoid unknown downloads\
- Remove unused programs\
- Reduce attack surface

**Narrator Transcript:** Every program installed creates potential risk.

Only use approved software.

Less software means fewer vulnerabilities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 12 -- Use Multi-Factor Authentication

**Slide Content:** - Protect email\
- Protect financial systems\
- Never approve unexpected prompts

**Narrator Transcript:** Multi-Factor Authentication is one of the
strongest protections available.

If you receive an unexpected prompt...

Do not approve it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 13 -- Backups Protect the Business

**Slide Content:** - Regular backups\
- Offline or cloud storage\
- Test restoration

**Narrator Transcript:** Backups protect against ransomware and
accidental deletion.

A backup that cannot be restored is not a backup.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 14 -- Limit Administrative Privileges

**Slide Content:** - Restrict admin rights\
- Separate admin accounts\
- Apply least privilege

**Narrator Transcript:** Not everyone needs administrator access.

Limiting admin privileges reduces damage potential.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 15 -- Warning Signs of an Incident

**Slide Content:** - Unexpected password resets\
- Strange sent emails\
- System abnormalities\
- Supplier payment changes

**Narrator Transcript:** If something feels wrong, report it.

Early reporting reduces damage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 16 -- What To Do Immediately

**Slide Content:** - Stop\
- Disconnect\
- Report\
- Preserve evidence

**Narrator Transcript:** Stop.

Disconnect the device.

Report immediately.

Do not delete evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# QUIZ SECTION

## SLIDE 17 -- Question 1

You receive an email saying a supplier's bank details have changed.

A)  Pay immediately\
B)  Reply to confirm\
C)  Call using a known phone number\
D)  Ignore it

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 18 -- Answer 1

Correct Answer: C

Always verify bank detail changes using a trusted phone number.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 19 -- Question 2

You receive repeated MFA approval notifications you did not request.

A)  Approve to stop the notifications\
B)  Ignore them\
C)  Deny and report immediately\
D)  Restart your computer

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 20 -- Answer 2

Correct Answer: C

Repeated prompts may indicate an attacker attempting access.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 21 -- Question 3

You receive an unexpected attachment from a known contact.

A)  Open it immediately\
B)  Forward to colleagues\
C)  Verify before opening\
D)  Delete without reporting

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 22 -- Answer 3

Correct Answer: C

Accounts can be compromised. Always verify suspicious attachments.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 23 -- Question 4

Your computer asks to install updates.

A)  Delay indefinitely\
B)  Install when convenient\
C)  Disable updates\
D)  Ignore permanently

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 24 -- Answer 4

Correct Answer: B

Delaying updates increases exposure to known vulnerabilities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 25 -- Question 5

Files suddenly become inaccessible and display a ransom note.

A)  Pay immediately\
B)  Disconnect and report\
C)  Ignore it\
D)  Reboot repeatedly

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SLIDE 26 -- Answer 5

Correct Answer: B

Disconnect immediately and report to reduce spread.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
