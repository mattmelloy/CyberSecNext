## Slide 4: Top 3 Ways Our Business Can Be Compromised  
1. **Phishing & scam emails/texts** (most common entry point)  
2. **Business Email Compromise (BEC)** – fake urgent payment requests  
3. **Ransomware & malware** – locks files and demands money  

**Presenter script (≈3 minutes):**  
“The ACSC says the top three ways small businesses get hit are:  
First—phishing. You get an email or text that looks real: ‘Your invoice is attached’ or ‘Click here to update your banking details.’ One click and they’re inside.  
Second—Business Email Compromise, or BEC. Criminals hack or spoof an email that looks like it’s from the boss, a supplier, or a customer saying ‘Change the bank details for this invoice’ or ‘Pay this urgent supplier now.’ Last year 15% of business reports involved actual money lost this way.  
Third—ransomware and other malware. Once inside, bad software locks our files and demands Bitcoin. Paying doesn’t guarantee you get your files back, and the ACSC strongly advises against it.  
These three account for the vast majority of problems we see in Australia right now.”

## Slide 5: How to Spot Trouble – Red Flags  
- Urgent language or pressure to act fast  
- Strange email addresses or slight spelling mistakes  
- Requests to pay, change details, or click links/attachments  
- Computer suddenly slow, pop-ups, or files you don’t recognise  
- “You’ve won a prize” or “Your account is suspended”  

**Presenter script (≈2 minutes):**  
“So how do you know something’s wrong? Here are the simple signs the ACSC wants every one of us to watch for:  
If an email feels pushy—‘Do this NOW or we lose the deal’—that’s a huge red flag.  
Check the sender’s address carefully. Real Aussie Post doesn’t come from gmail.com.  
If your computer starts acting strangely—slow, weird pop-ups, files you didn’t create—stop and tell someone immediately.  
Think of it like spotting a dodgy tradie at your front door. If it doesn’t feel right, don’t let them in!”

## Slide 6: What to Do the Moment You Spot a Problem  
1. **STOP** – don’t click anything else  
2. Unplug the computer from the network (or turn Wi-Fi off)  
3. Tell your manager or designated person straight away  
4. Call the bank if money is involved  
5. Report to ACSC via ReportCyber or 1300 CYBER 1 (1300 292 371)  

**Presenter script (≈2 minutes):**  
“If you see any of those red flags, here’s the exact 5-step plan the ACSC recommends:  
One: Stop. Close the email, don’t click or download.  
Two: Disconnect the device from the internet—pull the cable or turn Wi-Fi off. This stops the attack spreading.  
Three: Tell your manager or the person we’ve nominated right now—no matter how small it seems.  
Four: If money or banking is involved, ring the bank immediately on the number on the back of your card.  
Five: We will report it to the ACSC so they can help other businesses too.  
We have a simple one-page emergency plan printed and in the office—grab a copy if you need it.”

## Slide 8: Essential Eight in Plain English (Part 1)  
- Use **strong, unique passwords** + turn on **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)** everywhere possible  
- Keep **all software and apps up to date** (enable automatic updates)  
- **Back up** important files regularly and **test** the backups  

**Presenter script (≈2.5 minutes):**  
“Let’s make the Essential Eight super simple.  
First—strong passwords and MFA. Use a password manager (or at least a notebook in the safe) and make every password different. Then turn on that extra text code or app approval—called Multi-Factor Authentication. It’s like having a second lock on your door. Do this first on email, banking, and Xero/MYOB.  
Second—keep everything updated. Those updates fix the holes criminals use. Turn on automatic updates on your phone, computer, and every app.  
Third—back up your files. The ACSC says back up at least weekly to an external drive or the cloud, and test that you can actually open the backup. If ransomware hits, your backup is your get-out-of-jail-free card.”

---

## Slide 9: Essential Eight in Plain English (Part 2)  
- Limit who has **admin rights** (don’t use admin account for daily work)  
- Be careful with **email attachments and macros**  
- Only run **approved software** and keep devices physically secure  

**Presenter script (≈2 minutes):**  
“Continuing the Essential Eight:  
Don’t give everyone admin rights—only the people who really need them. Using a normal account every day is like not giving the spare house key to everyone in the street.  
Be wary of Word and Excel macros—only enable them if you know and trust the document.  
Only install software from trusted sources, and keep laptops locked in drawers or with a cable when you’re not at your desk. Simple physical security still matters!”
